## Milad's decision on round 1

Concept 3, Line Z, "was not bad". Brainstorm another round from it.

## What round 1's Line Z got wrong

1. At 16 px the small cut had only four lines with no step in the middle, so it read as stripes, not a Z.
2. Square pieces arranged into a Z is close to Blocks (five blocks cut from a tile).

## Variants

Files: `marks/<variant>-<master|small>-<light|dark>.svg`, generated by `python3 tools/marks.py 2 round-2/marks`. Every small cut now keeps five lines: 3, 2, 2, 2 and 3 px tall with 1 px gaps.

| 2B | Sliced | A true Z outline cut into five lines; the three diagonal slices keep the slant of the Z. | Middle slice | Reads as a Z at every size; lines second. |
| 2C | Return | Only three parts: two log lines and the carriage return (the diagonal) between them. | The return (diagonal) | Holds, but the amber diagonal looks light next to the ink bars. |
| 2D | Tagged lines | Round 1's amber level stripe in front of an all-ink Line Z. | Level stripe | Busiest; stripe plus five lines in 16 px is a lot. |

All four are Z marks, so in the lockup the mark is the inline Z of "ZIO" (family rule Q1). 2D puts the stripe before that Z.

## Recommendation

**2B Sliced.** It keeps what Milad liked about Line Z (a Z made of log lines, one amber line) and fixes both problems: the Z silhouette is intact, so it reads at 16 px, and the slanted cuts make it lines through a letter, not blocks stacked into one, which keeps it apart from Blocks.

Clear-space unit if chosen: one line height (4 of 24 units, one sixth of the mark height) on every side.

Palette and contrast are unchanged from round 1 (see `../round-1/NOTES.md`); every text pair passes 4.5:1.
